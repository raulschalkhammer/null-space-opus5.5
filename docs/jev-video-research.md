# Jev Short Films: Research, Pipeline and Asset Plan

Working title: **"The Model That Doesn't Talk"**, an anthology of playful 2D animated shorts about Jev (TypeSafe AI's "System One" model).

Direction: **not an explainer with visuals, but little films.** Characters, places, plot, gags and sound design carry the ideas. Each short secretly *acts out* one piece of mathematics, then ends with a brief "math card" that names what you just watched. Built with JavaScript/TypeScript; hand-drawn or free assets are welcome where code isn't the best tool.

Research date: 24 Sep 2026. Jev launched 15 Sep 2026, so facts are young. The primary TypeSafe pages (typesafe.ai, docs.typesafe.ai) weren't reachable from the research environment; the facts come from search snippets of those pages plus secondary coverage. **Verify every claim that appears on screen against the official docs.** Several "Jev guide" sites in search results are SEO aggregators, not authorities.

---

## 0. Chosen direction (update): Paper Diorama, narrated

After a first cartoon cut and three style frames, the series is going with **Paper Diorama**: layered cut paper lit like a stop-motion set, soft cast shadows, shallow depth of field, an earthy palette, and a calm narrator. Characters are objects, not cartoons: Gab is a faceless paper locomotive-typewriter, Jev is a lighthouse.

**How the math lives in the story (the house rules):**

1. **One visual rule: probability is width.** A track, a beam or a pennant is exactly as wide as the chance it carries. Every short reuses this, so the audience learns it once.
2. **Operations are physical.** A fork splits the width (a distribution). A brass marble dropped into a fork picks a branch with chance equal to its width (sampling). Riding a branch keeps only its share, so the track under Gab keeps thinning (multiplication, the chain rule).
3. **Show, then count, then name.** The picture comes first, then numbers printed on paper cards in the scene (`× 0.38`), and only then the symbol (`∏ p(word | words before it)`) with its name.
4. **Honest scale.** Widths are true to the numbers. When the track gets too thin to see, the film cuts to a "magnified 37×" view instead of cheating the width.
5. **Narration carries meaning, the picture carries intuition.** Subtitles are burned in; no floating labels that don't exist in the world.

Short no. 1 (paper cut) lives in `src/shorts/paper-track/`, with the narration script in `story/track-layer-vo.json` (voiced with Kokoro-82M, Apache-2.0, generated by `data-scripts/make-vo.py`).

## 2. The creative concept

### 2.1 The cast
- **Jev**: small, quiet, quick. **Never speaks.** Communicates only with glowing typed tags (`0.91`, `technical ✓`, a tiny triangle dial on its chest), chimes and body language. Think WALL-E or a Pixar short lead. The title *is* its character: the model that doesn't talk.
- **Gab**: a lovable, over-confident chatterbox (a typewriter-bodied creature or a parrot with a ticker-tape tail). Talks in one word at a time, always "100% sure", means well. The stand-in for a conventional LLM. **Not a villain**: the anthology ends with the two as partners.
- **Rosa**: a tired human night-shift worker (built from Open Peeps parts). The person whose life the decisions touch.
- Supporting: villagers, a carnival barker, a tailor. All cheap to build from CC0 parts.

Brand caution: characters are original and independent. Don't use TypeSafe's logo or imitate its branding.

### 2.2 Format
- **5 shorts × 60 to 120 s**, each followed by a **15 to 25 s "math card"** (a chalkboard freeze where a chalk hand writes the equation the short just acted out, with objects from the scene sliding into the symbols).
- Total ≈ 10 to 12 min, and **each short stands alone** for social clips. Frame at 16:9 but keep key action inside a centered 9:16 safe area so vertical recuts are cheap.
- House rule shown in the opening card: **"Every number in these films is real."** Real API outputs and real data drive the props, and vendor claims wear a little "vendor claim" stamp.

### 2.3 The five shorts

| # | Title | Plot (one breath) | The hidden math | Real data driving it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Track Layer** | Gab is a train that must lay its own track one piece at a time toward the station called *Answer*, picking each piece by spinning a weighted prize wheel. One unlucky spin and it derails into a swamp. Meanwhile Jev is already at the station holding up `0.91`. | Autoregression $p(y\mid x)=\prod_t p(y_t\mid y_{<t},x)$: sequential cost, each step conditioned on the last, errors compound. | Wheel slices are **real next-token probabilities** from a tiny local model (Transformers.js). |
| 2 | **The Mailroom** | Jev sorts a storm of letters into wall slots, several arms snapping at once. Gab tries to invent a new slot ("Miscellaneous-ish!"); the wall has no such slot. An ambiguous letter sends the dot on Jev's triangle dial to the middle; Jev rings a bell for Rosa. | Typed answer spaces (no schema hallucination), Choice as a point on the **probability simplex**, parallel independent questions $\prod_i p(a_i\mid s,q_i)$, Noul and Score as side gags. | Real Jev Choice outputs on real support messages (Banking77). |
| 3 | **The Forecasters** | A hilltop village has two weather forecasters. Gab says "100% sun!" every day; Jev says `70%`. The villagers drop a marble into a jar labeled with each forecast's number, marking rain or shine. Over a seasonal montage, Jev's jars fill to heights matching their labels; Gab's jars don't. | **Calibration**, reliability diagram (the jars *are* the diagram), ECE. | Jar heights = real reliability bins from our Jev run. |
| 4 | **The Honest Bowl** | Two training academies. Gab's coach rewards applause from an audience (RLHF). Jev's arena is a bowl: a marble for its stated odds always rolls to the true odds at the bottom; exaggerating pushes it up the wall, and a fog line marks the floor nobody can get below. | **Proper scoring rules**: $\mathbb{E}[(p-Y)^2]=(p-q)^2+q(1-q)$; RLHF vs RLCD. | The bowl is the actual Brier curve for a measured q. |
| 5 | **Night Shift** | 1,000 tickets flood Rosa's office at midnight. Jev handles most instantly; anything below a dial threshold lands in Rosa's inbox. Morning: she has a small stack, not a mountain. Twist: the office moves to a new town, the jars no longer line up, and a tailor re-fits Jev's dial. Final scene: Jev routes, Gab writes the replies. Partners. | Decision threshold $p>\tau^*=1-C_{\text{human}}/C_{\text{error}}$, risk-coverage, cost/latency race, **distribution shift and recalibration** (Molas' critique), System 1 + System 2. | Real Banking77 run: counts, accuracy, latency, cost; recalibration curve fitted on real labels. |

Full math reference for the writers and math cards is in §7.

## 3. Production pipeline (film-style, not slide-style)

1. **Script & beat sheet** per short (a page each). Gags first, math second; the math must be *visible in the action*.
2. **Storyboard**: thumbnails in [Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com) (MIT), [Krita](https://krita.org) (GPL, free) or paper and a phone camera.
3. **Animatic**: storyboard frames + scratch voice/SFX laid on the Remotion timeline. Lock timing here; it's the cheapest place to fix pacing.
4. **Design & build**: character sheets, cutout rigs, backgrounds, props.
5. **Data pass**: run the data scripts and commit JSON fixtures (§4.5). Props read from fixtures.
6. **Animation**: blocking → splining → polish, per shot.
7. **Sound**: dialogue (Gab only), foley, music, mix.
8. **Math cards**, titles, credits (with every asset license).
9. **Render & grade**; export 16:9 master + 9:16 recuts.

## 4. Toolchain

### 4.1 Engine: Remotion as the "film studio"

**Recommended: [Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev)** (React). The film format changes the pick versus the earlier explainer plan: shorts need a real editing timeline, many audio layers, image sequences, video clips, transitions and ready integrations for character formats, and Remotion has all of that.
- `<Sequence>` / `<Series>` = shots and scenes; `<Audio>` layering; `<OffthreadVideo>` and image sequences for hand-drawn clips.
- Official packages that matter for film work: `@remotion/rive` (rigged characters), `@remotion/lottie` (FX), `@remotion/transitions` (wipes, iris, etc.), `@remotion/motion-blur` (`<CameraMotionBlur>`, `<Trail>`), `@remotion/noise` (organic wobble, line boil, camera shake), `@remotion/paths` (write-on strokes, path morphs), `@remotion/three` (optional 3D), `@remotion/captions`.
- `spring()` and `interpolate()` give squash-and-stretch and overshoot for free.
- **License:** free for individuals and orgs of 3 people or fewer, commercial included. Beyond that, company license (about $25/creator seat/month). Keep all `remotion` and `@remotion/*` versions identical.

**Optional hybrid:** keep [Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io) (MIT) just for the math cards if you want its native LaTeX morphing, render them as clips, and drop them into Remotion with `<OffthreadVideo>`.

### 4.3 The "hand-made film" look, in code

| Technique | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Animating on twos** (the classic 2D cadence) | `const f = Math.floor(frame / 2) * 2;` and feed `f` into character animation, keeping camera moves on ones for smoothness. |
| **Line boil** (lines that shimmer like hand-inked cels) | An SVG filter `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` whose `seed` changes every 2 to 3 frames. Subtle scale (1 to 2 px). |
| **Sketchy props & math** | [Rough.js](https://roughjs.com) (MIT): `rough.generator()` → `generator.toPaths()` gives SVG path strings for jars, bowls, tracks, chalk diagrams. Regenerate with a new seed each held frame for boil. |
| **Squash & stretch, anticipation, overshoot** | `spring({ config: { damping, stiffness, mass } })` on scale/rotation; anticipation = a small negative move before the main one. |
| **Secondary motion** (ears, antennae, ticker tape) | Chain two springs with a lag, or a 2-point verlet rope baked to JSON. |
| **Blinks, breathing, idle** | `@remotion/noise` (`noise2D`) with a per-character seed so nobody is ever perfectly still. |
| **Camera** | A `<Camera>` wrapper applying translate/scale/rotate; **parallax** by multiplying camera offset per layer depth; `<CameraMotionBlur>` on fast whips; noise-driven shake on impacts. |
| **Physics gags** (marbles, letters, derailment) | [Matter.js](https://brm.io/matter-js/) or [planck.js](https://piqnt.com/planck.js/) (MIT) **simulated offline and baked to JSON**, then played back so every render is identical. Seed randomness with [seedrandom](https://github.com/davidbau/seedrandom). |
| **Lighting & mood** | Multiply/screen gradient overlays, a warm-cool palette per location (night shift = teal/amber), vignette. |
| **Film texture** | Paper texture overlay (ambientCG, CC0) + animated grain + a slight gate weave. |
| **Math that belongs to the world** | Equations as chalk, neon signs, embroidery on the tailor's tape, painted on the bowl. KaTeX renders to HTML/SVG; animate stroke write-ons with `@remotion/paths` (`evolvePath`). |

## 7. Math reference (for writers and math cards)

**Autoregression (Track Layer).** $p(y\mid x)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(y_t\mid y_{<t},x)$. One forward pass per token, sequential; latency ≈ TTFT + T·t_token. A decision probability would need $P(\text{yes})=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_\text{yes}}p(y\mid x)$ over every string that means yes, so in practice LLMs *say* a confidence, which is just more text. RLHF post-training is known to degrade calibration (GPT-4 Technical Report).

**Typed one-pass decisions (Mailroom).** $p(a_1,\dots,a_k\mid s)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p(a_i\mid s,q_i)$, $a_i\in\mathcal{A}_i$ declared up front, so there is no schema hallucination (semantic errors remain possible).
- Noul: Bernoulli $p\in[0,1]$.
- Choice: a point on the simplex $\Delta^{n-1}=\{p\ge 0,\ \sum_k p_k=1\}$; for 3 options, a triangle (corners = certainty, center = ignorance). Playful aside, labelled speculation: 255 is the largest value of one byte.
- Score: behaves like $\mathbb{E}[\ell]=\sum_\ell \ell\,p_\ell$, so it can land between levels.
- Confidence formula is undisclosed; illustrate the idea with normalized entropy $1-H(p)/\log n$ or top-2 margin, clearly labelled as illustrations.

**Calibration (Forecasters).** $P(Y=\hat y\mid \hat p=p)=p$. Reliability diagram: bin by confidence, plot accuracy. $\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_m \frac{|B_m|}{n}\,|\mathrm{acc}(B_m)-\mathrm{conf}(B_m)|$.

**Proper scoring (Honest Bowl).** Brier $=\frac1N\sum_i(p_i-y_i)^2$ (multi-class: sum over classes). If the true chance is $q$ and you report $p$: $\mathbb{E}[(p-Y)^2]=q(1-p)^2+(1-q)p^2=(p-q)^2+q(1-q)$, a bowl with its bottom at $p=q$ and an unbeatable floor $q(1-q)$. Log score: $-q\log p-(1-q)\log(1-p)$, also minimized at $p=q$. Murphy: Brier = reliability − resolution + uncertainty (always saying the base rate is calibrated but useless).
- RLHF: $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}[r_\phi(x,y)]-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_\text{ref})$, with $r_\phi$ a learned model of human preference (the applause meter).
- RLCD (as described): reward = negative proper score against verifiable truth (the bowl).

**Acting on decisions (Night Shift).** Automate when $(1-p)\,C_\text{error}<C_\text{human}$, i.e. $p>\tau^*=1-C_\text{human}/C_\text{error}$ (misroute $20, human triage $2 → τ* = 0.9). Only sound if $p$ is calibrated. Sweep τ for a risk-coverage curve.

**Recalibration (the tailor).** Calibration is data-relative (Molas). Fit on your own labels: Platt $\sigma(a z+b)$, temperature $\mathrm{softmax}(z/T)$, or isotonic regression, with $z=\log p$ (logit for Noul).
